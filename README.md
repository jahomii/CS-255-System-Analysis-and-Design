#CS 255 System Analysis and Design

DriverPass is a client who wanted a system that allowed users to purchase packages and make reservations for driving lessons. Their goal was to help users get their licenses by providing DMV accurate tests and in-person driving lessons with real instructors. I think I did particularly well when it came to analyzing the needs for DriverPass's system. 

If I were to revise a part of my documents, I think I would pick specifically my sequence diagram. I think that diagram in particular could've used more detail. I would depict more ways the the DriverPass system communicated with itself and show more uses for the admin such as downloading files. 

I interpreted the users' needs based on what I found in the documentation of their proposed system. There were specific functions that were stated outright, like the need to manually insert new packages and for users to make reservations. Based on these functions, you can then use reasoning skills to conclude that the admin and IT officer accounts should have special privileges to add packages, and the users should have no access to these privileges. Considering the users' needs is important when designing because the system you're making is for them to use, so if they can't use it in the way they need to, then they won't use it. 

I approach designing software similar to how I approach art. I hear the idea and gather as much information as I can before I begin executing. I gather together my sources, references, and inspirations and lay the groundwork for the idea. In this case, I looked at similar systems to see how they function after I read the admin's needs for the system. Then I wrote down what I found to be most important and effective as well as any new ideas that might benefit the system. I would use these strategies in the future to analyze and design a system as well as bring my current analysis to another person and hear their take on the idea. It is always good to get a second opinion.
